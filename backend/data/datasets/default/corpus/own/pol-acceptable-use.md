# Acceptable Use Policy

## Purpose

Company equipment and access are provided for company work. Use them carefully, respect other people's information and leave systems available for colleagues who need them. This policy applies in both offices, at home and when travelling. It covers company laptops, shared machines, email, internet use and information handled through company accounts.

People owns this policy. Managers should explain it during induction and answer ordinary questions about reasonable use. IT Support handles equipment and access problems. Security should be contacted where the concern is possible misuse, disclosure or an unexpected request involving an account.

## Looking after equipment

Use the company laptop assigned to you and take reasonable care of it. Keep food and drinks away from equipment where a spill could cause damage. Carry laptops securely when moving between locations. Do not leave equipment unattended in public areas or visible in an unattended vehicle.

Do not attempt a physical repair yourself. Report damage to the helpdesk and describe the problem clearly. IT Support will decide whether to repair or replace the laptop. If a replacement is issued, complete the handover and return the old laptop to IT. Do not lend the old laptop to a colleague or keep it for occasional use.

Do not install unapproved software or change security settings. If a tool is needed for work, explain the requirement to IT Support. Do not use someone else's administrative account to install it. A business need should be handled through the normal approval process rather than an improvised change.

## Accounts and shared machines

Use only your own account. Passwords, authenticator approvals and recovery codes must not be shared. A colleague's absence is not permission to sign in as them. Ask the relevant system owner to arrange approved access when work needs to be covered.

Shared machines are shared equipment, not shared identities. Sign out at the end of your session so the next user does not work under your account. Do not leave confidential material visible on the screen or local working documents available to the next person. Follow the shared device arrangements and tell the helpdesk if the listed users or location have changed.

Check the recipient before sending information and the audience before placing it in a shared area. All Staff access to the file share does not make all information suitable for general circulation. Use access controls and the system owner's guidance for confidential or restricted material.

## Email and internet use

Write company email with the same care as other business correspondence. Do not send abusive, discriminatory, threatening or deliberately misleading messages. Do not use company email to impersonate someone or to commit the company to arrangements you are not authorised to make.

Treat unexpected attachments and requests for passwords with caution. Never enter a password after clicking an email link. Open the normal sign-in route yourself if you need to check an account. Do not approve an MFA request you did not initiate. Report suspicious messages to Security without circulating them to colleagues for informal opinions.

Use internet access for lawful work and reasonable personal activity. Do not deliberately access unlawful material, attempt unauthorised access to other people's information or use company equipment to interfere with someone else's work. Avoid downloads that have no work purpose and could create security or capacity problems.

## Reasonable personal use

Limited personal use is allowed when it does not interfere with duties, create significant cost or expose company information. It must not become a separate business activity or prevent another person from using shared equipment. Managers may ask staff to reduce personal use where it affects work.

Personal phones may be used to read email. They are not registered company devices. This limited permission does not allow other internal systems to be accessed from those phones. Do not use personal storage or personal email to move company work between devices.

## Loss, mistakes and concerns

Report a lost device to the helpdesk the same day. Give the device name, when and where it was last seen and whether it was in use. Do not wait to finish searching before making contact. Provide further details when they become available.

Report accidental disclosure or possible account misuse promptly to Security. Explain what you observed and what you have already done. Do not conceal a mistake or make unsupported claims about its impact. Early, factual information helps the company decide what action is needed.

## Consequences and advice

The company may restrict access while suspected misuse is assessed. People will consider the circumstances, seriousness and any repeated conduct when deciding an appropriate response. Deliberate or serious misuse may lead to disciplinary action. Equipment problems and honest mistakes should still be reported promptly.

If a request seems inconsistent with this policy, ask your manager, People or Security before carrying it out. An urgent instruction does not remove the requirement to protect accounts and company information.
