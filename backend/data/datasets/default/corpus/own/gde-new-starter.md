# New Starter Guide

## Welcome and your first day

This guide covers the practical steps for your first week at the company. Your manager will explain the work and introduce the team. People can help with employment details, office arrangements and questions about who to contact. Jessica Moss (jessica.moss) leads HR and can direct a People question to the right person.

Start with your own account and company laptop. Do not borrow a colleague's account while waiting for access. If something is missing, tell your manager and the helpdesk so it can be arranged through the proper approval process.

## Collecting your laptop

IT Support will arrange collection of your company laptop and confirm your identity at handover. Check the device name with the analyst and keep it available for future support calls. Laptop names use LT-, your first initial and your surname in capitals. Maya Patel (maya.patel), for example, uses LT-MPATEL.

The laptop is assigned to you. Look after it, take it with you when leaving an unattended public area and report damage to the helpdesk. Do not attempt repairs yourself. A lost device must be reported to the helpdesk the same day, even if you are still searching for it.

## First sign-in and password

Staff sign in through the identity provider. The helpdesk will guide you through your first sign-in and any initial password change. Enter the password privately. The analyst needs to know whether sign-in worked, not what password you chose.

Use at least 14 characters. A passphrase made from unrelated words is encouraged. Avoid obvious phrases and personal details. The banned-password list may reject a weak choice; choose something substantially different if it does. Never share a password or reuse it across separate systems or personal accounts.

There is no forced periodic password change. Change it when exposure is suspected or Security directs you to do so. If you forget it, contact the helpdesk for a reset after identity verification. Do not ask a colleague to sign in for you.

## Set up MFA

Every account must use MFA with an authenticator app unless Security has approved a named exception. Follow the enrolment instructions through the intranet and the identity provider. Complete a test sign-in you started yourself before considering setup finished.

Generate the authenticator's one-time recovery codes during setup. Keep them private and secure, separate from the device used for the app. They are the fallback when the app cannot be used. Do not email them or include them in a helpdesk request. Only approve an MFA prompt for your own sign-in. Report an unexpected request to Security.

## Finding your work

The intranet is the place for staff guidance and company information. Ask your manager to show you the normal route to it. The file share holds working documents; ask which team area you should use and how its information should be handled. Email and intranet information are internal, while the file share is confidential.

All staff belong to All Staff, which provides baseline access to email, intranet and file share. Some roles need additional permissions. These must be approved by the system owner through the appropriate privileged group. Your department alone does not determine that access.

## During the first week

Check that you can complete ordinary work and raise missing access early. From outside the offices, use your company laptop and the VPN gateway for internal systems other than email and the identity provider. Personal phones may be used to read email only; they are not registered company devices.

Read the Acceptable Use Policy and ask your manager about anything unclear. Contact the helpdesk for sign-in or equipment problems, People for staff questions and Security for possible account misuse or suspicious email. Before travelling abroad for work, send Security a travel notice with the country, dates and company laptop details.
