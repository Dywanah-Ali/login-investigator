# Device Onboarding and Replacement

## Purpose and preparation

Use this procedure when issuing a company laptop to a starter or replacing a damaged laptop. IT Support owns the handover. The analyst must know who will receive the laptop, their department and office, and whether this is a first issue or a replacement. Do not give out a laptop against an unverified request.

Check the person's account arrangements before the appointment. The laptop handover does not approve additional access. All Staff is the baseline, and privileged membership needs system owner approval under the Access Control Policy. The access check is consistent with M1018: confirm the permissions needed for the role rather than copying a colleague's membership.

## Issue steps

1. **Identify the person and prepare the laptop.** Confirm the recipient against the starter or replacement request. Check that the laptop is ready for company use and that its physical condition is suitable. Resolve visible damage before handover. Do not ask the person to use an unprepared personal device while the company laptop is being arranged.

2. **Apply the naming rule.** A laptop name starts with LT-, followed by the person's first initial and surname in capitals. For example, Maya Patel (maya.patel) has LT-MPATEL. Use the spelling in the staff details and check the name before completing the device record. A replacement adds -R2 to the original laptop name. Do not invent another naming pattern to complete a request more quickly.

3. **Record the assignment.** Record the person, username, department, office, laptop name, issue date, time in UTC and the analyst. Explain that the laptop is assigned to one person. It must not be passed to a colleague without IT Support arranging the change. Confirm that the device record and the physical laptop refer to the same name.

4. **Support first sign-in.** Ask the person to sign in through the identity provider and complete the password and MFA steps privately. Verify that ordinary email, intranet and file share access works. Where approved privileged membership is required, check only the access that has been authorised. The analyst must not request the person's chosen password or recovery codes.

## Replacement steps

5. **Record the reason and both names.** Describe the fault in ordinary words. The handover record must include the recipient, original laptop name, replacement laptop name, exact handover time in UTC and analyst. James Carter (james.carter), for example, uses LT-JCARTER as the original name and LT-JCARTER-R2 as the replacement name. Do not record only the new name; the relationship between the two matters when later activity is checked.

6. **Take the original laptop back into IT custody.** The old laptop goes back to IT for repair. Record its condition and physical return. It is no longer the employee's working laptop after the replacement handover. If the original cannot be returned at the appointment, record the reason and arrange collection rather than marking it received.

7. **Complete the handover together.** The recipient confirms receipt of the replacement and the return of the original. The analyst confirms the names, time and condition. Both sign the handover record. Check that the person can use the replacement for normal work before closing the support request. Explain where to report any remaining problem.

## Shared machines and completion

MR-PC-01 and WH-TERM-01 are shared machines. Their locations and authorised users belong in the Shared Device Register. Do not treat either machine as a person's individually issued laptop. Report changes to their location or users to IT Support for review.

Before closure, confirm that the assignment, handover and any repair action agree. Device history must make clear which laptop a person was using at a particular time. A different name after a documented replacement is expected and should be explainable from the handover record.
