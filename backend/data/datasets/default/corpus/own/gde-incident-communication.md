# Incident Communication Template

## How to use this template

Use this template when staff need instructions during a security incident. Replace the square-bracketed fields with confirmed information. Remove an optional sentence if it does not apply. Do not fill a gap with an assumption. If a fact is unknown, say what is being checked and when an update will follow.

Security coordinates the wording with the owner of the affected system. Agree the audience and approval before sending. Use a known communication route that is suitable for the affected service. If email is affected, decide an alternative way to reach staff rather than relying on the unavailable route.

All dates must use YYYY-MM-DD or words. All times must explicitly say UTC. Describe what staff need to do in ordinary language. Do not include passwords, recovery codes or unnecessary personal details. This document is a blank communication aid and describes no actual event.

## Initial message

Subject: [Short description of the affected service and required staff action]

To: [Affected staff or department]

Issued: [YYYY-MM-DD] at [HH:MM] UTC

Message owner: [Responsible team]

We are investigating [confirmed issue in plain language]. This affects [system or activity] for [scope of affected staff]. We confirmed the issue at [HH:MM] UTC on [YYYY-MM-DD].

At present, [what is known about availability or access]. We are checking [specific unresolved point]. We cannot yet confirm [unknown fact that matters to staff].

Please [primary action staff should take]. If you are currently [relevant working situation], [specific instruction]. For urgent work involving [affected activity], contact [established team contact route] for guidance.

Our next update will be issued on [YYYY-MM-DD] by [HH:MM] UTC through [agreed communication route]. We will send that update even if there is no material change.

For questions or to provide relevant observations, contact [team and established contact route]. Include [useful non-sensitive details] and the time in UTC. Do not send passwords, authenticator codes or recovery codes.

## Progress update

Subject: Update on [affected service or activity]

Issued: [YYYY-MM-DD] at [HH:MM] UTC

Since our previous message, [confirmed change or statement that the position is unchanged]. The current effect on staff is [plain description of impact].

Please continue to [required action], or begin to [new instruction]. This instruction applies to [audience or location]. We are still checking [outstanding question].

The next update will be issued on [YYYY-MM-DD] by [HH:MM] UTC. Contact [team and established route] if [condition requiring staff contact].

## Service restoration message

Subject: [Service or activity] available again

Issued: [YYYY-MM-DD] at [HH:MM] UTC

[Service or activity] is available for [confirmed scope] from [HH:MM] UTC on [YYYY-MM-DD]. Staff may now [approved action]. Before resuming work, please [any required check].

[Outstanding limitation, or confirmation that no staff-facing limitation is known]. If you cannot complete [normal activity], contact [support route] and describe [details needed].

## Final check before sending

Confirm that the message agrees with the current operational position. Check the audience, service names, instructions and next update time. Make sure someone owns the promised update. Remove unused placeholders and speculative explanations. Ask the approving person to check that staff can understand the action without needing access to internal technical notes.
