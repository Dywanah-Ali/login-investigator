# Password Policy

## Purpose

A password protects an individual account. It must be difficult to guess and must not be available to another person through sharing or reuse. This policy applies to every company account and to staff in both offices and working remotely. Version 3.0 replaces version 2.0 dated 2024-02-19. Use this version for password creation, changes and helpdesk resets from 2026-06-01.

Long, unique passwords are the company's standard. Routine calendar changes are no longer required. Change a password when there is reason to suspect exposure, and follow instructions from Security when it directs a change. Do not use a previous policy's routine change timetable for current accounts.

## Choosing a password

Passwords must contain at least 14 characters. Passphrases are encouraged because several unrelated words can be easier to remember than a short complicated string. Choose words that do not form a familiar quotation, a common expression or a description of your own life. A name followed by a date is not a suitable passphrase.

Do not build passwords from your username, department, the company email domain or information colleagues and customers would know. Adding a punctuation mark to a common password does not make it acceptable. Length matters, but a long predictable phrase can still be guessed.

Password creation and changes must be checked against the banned-password list. The list includes commonly guessed passwords and passwords known to have been exposed. If a proposed password is rejected, choose a substantially different phrase. Do not keep adding a number until a slight variation is accepted. IT maintains the check with Security's oversight.

## One password for one use

Never share a password with a colleague, manager, helpdesk analyst or anyone claiming to provide urgent support. This includes reading it over the phone, sending it in email or allowing another person to watch you type it. An authorised support task does not require the analyst to know your usual password.

Do not reuse passwords across systems. Do not use a company password for personal services or a personal password for company work. Where a system directs you to the identity provider, use the normal company sign-in process. This is central authentication; it is not an instruction to copy the same password into separate system accounts.

Do not leave a password on paper beside a laptop or on a shared desk. Do not put it in the file share, an email draft or an ordinary note. If you cannot manage a password safely, contact the helpdesk for guidance before choosing an insecure workaround.

## Changes after suspected exposure

Tell Security promptly if you think someone else has learned your password, or if you entered it into an unfamiliar prompt. Use a known contact route. Do not continue interacting with the message or page that raised the concern. Security will decide whether further account checks are needed.

Change the affected password through the normal company route or ask the helpdesk to assist. Choose a new, unrelated password. If the exposed password was used elsewhere, make the other passwords unique as well. A password change alone does not establish that an account is safe; Security may also require sessions to be ended and access to be reviewed.

## Helpdesk resets

If you forget your password, contact the helpdesk. The analyst must verify your identity before resetting it, using established staff details and a known contact route. Urgency, seniority and knowledge of a colleague's name do not replace verification. The analyst records the checks completed without recording passwords or recovery codes.

After verification, the analyst performs the reset and gives instructions for setting a new password privately. Any temporary password is for the reset process only. Select a compliant password and confirm that sign-in works before the request is closed. Do not send the chosen password to the analyst as proof.

## Responsibilities

Staff follow these rules and report possible exposure. IT Support applies the verification procedure consistently and records the outcome of resets. Security reviews the banned-password requirements and handles suspected account misuse. System owners must raise any system limitation with Security rather than silently accepting weaker passwords. MFA remains a separate requirement under the Multi-Factor Authentication Policy.
