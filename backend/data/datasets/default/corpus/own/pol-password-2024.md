# Password Policy

## Purpose and application

This policy sets the company's password requirements. All staff must follow it when creating or changing passwords for company accounts. Managers must make sure their teams understand the requirements before access is provided. IT Support is responsible for helping people complete password changes and for resolving problems with password entry.

A password belongs to one person. It must not be shared to cover leave, speed up work or allow a colleague to use a system without their own account. Requests for additional access must follow the access approval process. Permission to do a task does not include permission to use someone else's password.

## Password composition

Every password must be at least 8 characters long and include an upper case letter, a lower case letter, a digit and a symbol. All four types are required. A password which meets the length rule but omits one of these types is not acceptable.

Do not choose your name, username, department or an easily guessed word with a single digit added. Avoid dates and information known to colleagues. A password should be difficult for someone else to predict even if they know your job and have seen your public contact details. Choose a value you can remember without displaying it at your desk.

Do not use the same password for separate systems or for a personal account. If one password becomes known, other accounts must not be exposed by the same value. Where company sign-in is handled by the identity provider, follow that sign-in route rather than creating an unnecessary separate account.

## Scheduled changes

Passwords must be changed every 90 days. Complete a change when prompted; do not delay it until you next need a system urgently. IT Support can explain the change process if a prompt is unclear. Use the normal sign-in route and do not follow an unexpected email link to make the change.

The last 5 passwords may not be reused. Select a new password rather than cycling through previous choices. Small predictable variations, such as changing only the final digit, are discouraged. The new value must meet the composition requirements each time.

Change a password immediately if you believe someone else knows it. A scheduled change date is not a reason to wait. Tell Security about the possible exposure so that it can assess whether further action is required.

## Failed sign-ins

An account is locked after 5 failed sign-in attempts. The lockout lasts 15 minutes. Check the username, keyboard settings and password before trying again after that period. Do not ask someone else to sign in on your behalf while you wait.

Contact the helpdesk if you cannot complete sign-in or are unsure why it failed. The analyst must verify your identity before carrying out a password reset. Knowing the account name or requesting a reset from a familiar-looking email address is not sufficient by itself.

## Safe handling and support

Do not send a password through email or place it in the file share. Never include it in a helpdesk request. An analyst may ask you to confirm that a change worked, but must not ask you to disclose the new password. Enter passwords privately, including when using a shared machine.

If a temporary password is issued during a verified reset, use it only to complete the reset and select your own password. Confirm successful access with the helpdesk without repeating the password. IT Support records who requested the reset and the checks performed.

Managers must allow time for staff to resolve password problems properly. Security deals with suspected exposure and may require further changes. Failure to follow these requirements may lead to access being restricted while the problem is addressed.
