# Remote Access and VPN Policy

## Scope

This policy covers access to company systems from outside the UK office and the US office. Remote access must use the approved sign-in route and a company laptop. Working from home does not change the permissions a person has, the sensitivity of information or the requirement to protect it from other people nearby.

Staff may sign in to email and the identity provider from home on a company laptop. Access to other internal systems from outside the offices must go through the VPN gateway. Use a company laptop for those connections. Permission to connect does not confer permission to administer the VPN gateway or use systems outside your approved access groups.

## Starting a remote session

Use your own company account and the company laptop issued to you. Start the VPN connection through the normal company setup, then complete sign-in and the required MFA check. If the authenticator app cannot be used, follow the recovery-code procedure in the Multi-Factor Authentication Policy.

Check that the connection is established before opening another internal system. If it cannot be established, contact the helpdesk. Do not move company information to a personal device or ask a colleague to send restricted material through another route to get around the connection problem.

All Staff membership allows ordinary use of the VPN gateway. Administrative access to the VPN gateway is restricted to IT Admins and is subject to system owner approval. Leah Gibbons (leah.gibbons) owns the VPN gateway. Questions about its configuration must go through IT Infrastructure rather than being resolved by changing settings without approval.

## Addresses and attribution

The VPN gateway assigns addresses from a small shared pool in turn. Several people can use the same address during a week. An address identifies a session in context; it is not a permanent label for a person or laptop.

When investigating remote access, record the session time in UTC, the account and the device as well as the address shown in the connection records. Compare the relevant time periods before connecting activity to a person. Two accounts using an address at different times are not evidence that the people shared credentials or a device.

Support staff must explain this limitation when passing information to Security. Do not identify someone solely from an address copied from a separate day's record. If the session cannot be matched confidently, say that attribution is unresolved and request the missing information.

## Working abroad

File a travel notice with Security before travelling abroad for work. Include the destination country, travel dates, purpose, company laptop name and the manager's approval. Security acknowledges the notice so that expected overseas sign-ins can be checked against it.

Tell Security if the country, dates or laptop changes. A notice for one trip does not cover later journeys. A confirmed trip can explain a new country in a sign-in record, but it does not excuse unexpected devices or activity outside the travel period. Security may contact the traveller through a known route to clarify those details.

Use the same VPN requirements abroad as at home. Take reasonable care when working where others can see the screen. Do not leave the company laptop unattended in a public place. Report loss to the helpdesk the same day and give the last known location and time.

## Personal phones

Personal phones may be used to read email only. They are not company devices and are not registered. This permission does not authorise access to the intranet, file share or any other internal system from a personal phone. Do not copy attachments into personal services or use a personal phone as a substitute for a company laptop.

Protect email on the phone from casual access by others. Report suspected exposure through the normal support or Security contact route. When checking email activity, an unregistered personal phone may be consistent with this limited permission; it must not be treated as a registered company laptop.

## Ending work and support

End remote sessions when work is finished. Keep the laptop secure between sessions and follow IT instructions for maintenance. Report connection problems to the helpdesk with the time in UTC and the laptop name. Never include passwords or recovery codes. IT Infrastructure owns the connection process, and Security handles questions about possible account misuse.
